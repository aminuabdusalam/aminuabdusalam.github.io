Okay, let me use this space to pour out some thoughts about my career.

I like being in a position where I know just enough about everything to connect the pieces. Maybe that is why I have never found a title that checks every box for me. At least, maybe not until now.

Let's start with what I actually want.

I want to influence **what** is being done to solve a problem and **how** it is being done.

That is why I chose the product management route after my internship, even though several people told me I would make a good developer. Do I sometimes think about what might have happened if I had followed the engineering path? Of course. I probably would have developed much deeper technical expertise by now.

At the same time, I have enjoyed my role precisely because it puts me close to both sides. I get to understand the problem, influence the direction, and stay involved in how the solution comes together.

Three promotions in less than three years are a testament to the fact that I know my stuff. And let's not lie, I grind. What is worth doing is worth doing well.

But even with that recognition, I sometimes feel like there is still a lot of potential I have not unlocked.

Should I move further into the *what* and *why*? Maybe, but how deeply do I care about operating exclusively in that space?

Should I move further into the *how*, especially system design and architecture? Probably, but how much time can I dedicate to developing that depth alongside everything else already on my plate?

Whenever these thoughts come up, I seem to end up in the same cycle.

Then I started wondering: what if there is a role that does not require me to move completely to either side?

The recent conversation around the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role got my attention. More recently, Technical Deployment Lead (TDL) has clicked with me even more strongly. Both made me think, *Wait, this might actually describe how I want to work.*

In the last few days, I have also realized that Technical Program Manager (TPM) may have been closer to what I was already doing than I wanted to admit. I pushed back on identifying with it because the title felt too long and somehow still sounded like someone asking me to pick a side.

Yes, I recognize the irony that FDE and TDL are not exactly short names either. Let's blame recency bias for why I am suddenly willing to accept those.

So perhaps the question is whether I should accept that there is already a title for the kind of work I enjoy, whichever one it is, or intentionally move further toward one side.

If I had to choose today, the technical side would probably win.

Not because I want to leave product thinking behind. Product judgment is already part of how I operate. Instead, deeper technical capability feels like the larger area of untapped growth. AI-assisted tools also make that depth more accessible to build without giving up the product instincts I already rely on.

Maybe the answer is not choosing between product and engineering. Maybe it is becoming much better at building while continuing to think like a product person.

Well, I finally got the thoughts out.
